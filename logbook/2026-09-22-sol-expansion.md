All twenty reports now have both readings saved. The run produces 540 topic judgments, 134 selected topic-evidence records, and 190 actor/office records with 348 statements. All 231 positive topic quotations, 134 topic-evidence quotations and 348 actor quotations occur exactly in the supplied source segments. Two topic judgments remain unresolved. Sixteen obligation answers in one batch use yes/no; the offline comparison explicitly normalizes those strings to 1/0, retains the raw values, and marks the normalization. No extra inference was used to repair formatting.

The broad topic mapping matches 84 of 98 available original human references and detects 43 of 47 positives. Affordability/displacement matches 20/20 and character/scale/preservation 19/20. Infrastructure/services remains weaker at 14/20, including only 4/7 original positives. These are agreements with coarse and imperfect historical codes, not accuracy estimates. Council involvement matches 19/20, with the applicant-role error discussed below accounting for the missed positive. Detailed Council stance differs more often because a concern, component-specific opposition and overall support are different claims.

This expansion tests the agreed Sol-medium procedure on twenty additional human-coded reports. It preserves the 27 topic definitions and supplies a separate short actor-inventory instruction, with two reports per reader job. Readers receive the frozen source packets but no human answers, selection strata, previous model answers or one another's outputs. Completed readings survive the usage interruption; only jobs without saved answers are restarted. Jev and corpus processing remain paused.

The source archive and the existing pilot task contain the frozen protocol, prompts, codebook, original human references, selection roster, ten source-packet batches and all raw readings. Selection uses a fixed hash rank among 122 eligible human-coded reports and excludes the earlier September 22 Sol/Luna source bundles. It deliberately includes Council and civic positions, long issue-positive reports and a remaining-report group. Nine reports have multiple earlier Jev request parts. Older development history is flagged, not excluded; this is targeted validation, not a representative or untouched holdout.

The actor controls precede the twenty-report test. An initial clarification caused the Bartow reader to omit the proposed operator. The final instruction explicitly retains applicants and operators outside the independent-civic category and was rechecked on Bartow. Flatbush/Caton's paired-report check uses the first clarification; its paired-source language did not change. Both attempts remain visible. These known controls cannot establish performance on new reports.

The most important source finding is independent of model choice. Four reports contain scanned recommendation attachments missing from their supplied text: Bronx Special Districts (PDF pages 38--52), 3276 Jerome Avenue (8--11), 19 East 72nd Street (13--21), and Variety Boys and Girls Club (12--18, beginning with a map). Page images confirm substantive recommendation material in each case. A Rose Plaza companion also omits its final zoning-map page. A check of all 28 source PDFs records page counts, omitted-page lists and hashes; it is not a visual audit of every page. The extraction helper limits partial-page OCR to pages at or before the first CPC resolution. Consequently scanned attachments can disappear even when the main narrative has abundant selectable text and is labeled usable.

The missing-attachment finding is documented without altering frozen packets halfway through the experiment. Docling was unavailable for the diagnostic page conversion; the fallback text remained empty, and rendered page images established that the content was present. Before broader inference, the next source repair must recover scanned attachments and preserve application attribution. A full-supplied-segment declaration is insufficient evidence of full-PDF coverage.

Source review also identifies narrower model and measurement issues. Bay Terrace's councilmember is the applicant; the inventory keeps his testimony but its single role is applicant, so the Council aggregate misses him. St. George treats an ordinary provision of the enacted special-district zoning text as a specific obligation, despite the codebook's main-action exclusion. Plaza 75 treats a favorable description of school seats as a concern. Sunset Park's concerns prompting the proposal are recovered, but concern-only does not map to support or opposition. A separate broad involvement indicator implements Jacob's earlier decision that this counts as involvement; it does not change the frozen stance scores.

Other discrepancies are useful recovered evidence or differences from the old coding. Canarsie's preservation organization explicitly speaks in favor while requesting changes; the old code says opposition. St. George has both a supporting economic-development organization and an opposing civic association. Variety Boys and Girls Club is the applicant, so its statements are retained without being counted as independent civic support. Member testimony must remain distinct from an organization's authorized stance, and support for aspects of a proposal need not mean overall support. Original answers and human codes are not overwritten by these post-reading judgments.

The appended findings give the saved-response checks and human-code comparisons. Exact quotations verify source presence, not correct interpretation, full actor coverage or completeness of extracted PDF text. Broad historical labels do not independently validate all detailed discussion, concern and obligation decisions. The selected source-review observations are manager judgments after reading model answers, not new gold labels.

The existing task Makefile reproduces selection, comparisons, source reviews and data reports from saved files without launching model jobs. The logbook Makefile links both generated findings into this document. The 204 earlier pilot outputs remain byte-for-byte unchanged, and the eighteen frozen design/prompt/packet hashes remain unchanged. Final checks include the task build, unchanged dry runs, parallel missing-output regeneration, source/evidence validation and whitespace checks. Per-run dollar costs are unavailable for these Codex subagents; no API or Jev calls were made.
