---
title: "Jev's free CPC trial is promising for screening, but revisions remain unreliable"
date: "September 19, 2026"
author: "Codex research record"
---

The Jev trial incurred no observed Vercel credit charge. It recovered nearly
all prepared requests despite frequent HTTP 503 service failures. On the
reports that can be compared as complete bundles, Jev performs similarly to
the earlier Sol pilot overall, with considerable differences across fields.
Traffic and local-request fields look useful for further testing. Revisions,
responses to local pressure, and infrastructure concerns need more work.
The generated tables below preserve the denominators and separate original
human references from AI-assisted reconciled working values.

High exact agreement does not establish coverage of rare concerns. Jev missed
two of the four affordability/displacement positives in the working reference
and five of seven infrastructure positives. The Council-member comparison
contains only one substantive position; the civic-group comparison contains
four. Both lack human-coded support/request examples in the comparable set.
These results cannot yet validate the detailed actor fields or establish
reliable coverage of positive cases. A high-confidence filter helps the pooled
comparison but does not solve revisions: only four of eight revision labels
with confidence at least 0.90 match the working reference.

Disagreement also does not automatically mean model error. An unblinded source
check of `C 860553 ZMM`, PDF pages 3--5, finds explicit CPC concern about
development pressure on the Apollo and Loews theatres and a signed restrictive
declaration limiting FAR and specifying streetwall height. Jev and Sol both
code character and revision positively while the existing human values are
zero. Their reading is defensible under our broad CPC-or-local issue and
commitment definitions. In `C 820683 MMX`, page 2 and the maintenance letters
on pages 5--10 document civic organizations' maintenance commitments, also
supporting a model-positive civic reading. Conversely, `C 900211 ZSQ`, pages
4--8, mainly describes an already completed church conversion, existing
sound-attenuating walls, and standard permit compliance; Jev's low-confidence
revision positive appears to overread that language. These are diagnostic
examples, not a new systematic adjudication, and no labels were replaced.

The trial reuses the frozen 30-report Sol development sample, all focal and
included companion pages, and the 31 non-count fields. The revised Jev wording
incorporates the earlier character/revision discussion, so this is not a
controlled comparison of models under identical prompts. Four long bundles
were split without truncation and are retained as part-level answers pending
joint review. One unsplit report, `C 030300 ZMK`, remained unavailable after
five recorded attempts. These cases remain in the comparison dataset with
explicit statuses, rather than becoming negative findings or disappearing.

The next useful test would separate substantive adopted changes from routine
permit conditions and evaluate frozen questions on additional already-coded
reports with more positive affordability and actor examples. Jev can be a
cheap screening layer, but this trial does not justify treating its complete
output as validated research data. Counts remain with regex/Codex, and verified
evidence selection and combination of long-report parts remain unfinished.
