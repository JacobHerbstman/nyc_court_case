The Sol subagent pilot produces useful additional coding, particularly for local requests, but it does not justify treating every contextual field as settled. On completed, nonconflicting human values, Sol matches local requests in 27 of 27 reports, compared with 21 of 27 for the current regex. Affordability/displacement matches rise from 24 to 26 of 29. Character/scale/preservation remains difficult: Sol matches 16 of 26, compared with 13 for regex. Revisions/concessions match 19 of 26, compared with 17. These are development comparisons within an intentionally selected human-coded sample, not accuracy estimates for all CPC reports. The appended table retains the field-specific denominators and other outcomes.

Jacob authorized Sol subagents instead of paid API calls. Three fresh readers used `gpt-5.6-sol` at medium reasoning for 30 primary reports and three independent repeats. Ten primary reports have completed Jacob coding only, ten Tyler coding only, and ten both, balanced across available decades from the 1970s through the 2020s. The older 20-report API pilot and overlapping source bundles are excluded. Packets contain full extracted text from 56 source reports, including companions. The comparison therefore changes both the method and available context. Human answers and regex predictions were withheld from packets; blinding relied on instructions within a shared filesystem.

Some measured differences reflect the underlying coding definitions. On their respective 20-report completed samples, Sol agrees with Jacob on revisions/concessions in 18 reports but with Tyler in 11. Those samples overlap only partly, so the difference is not a clean comparison of coders. On the three repeated reports, independent Sol readers agree on 96 of 105 field labels. One reader treats coordinated auction timing as a development revision and scale issue; the other treats it as an administrative condition. In another case, the readers code a companion report's tenant relocation commitment differently as displacement and as a concession. Jacob's existing coding distinguishes the displacement issue from a revision/concession in that case. These examples point toward clarifying the difference between the original proposal, a concern discussed in review, and a substantive change made during review. No pilot answer or human judgment was overwritten to settle these differences.

## Follow-up: what character and revision disagreements mean

The low agreement combines some model overreach with a mismatch in coding definitions and some questionable human zeros. The follow-up inspected every completed, nonconflicting disagreement in the two fields: 17 field values across 12 reports. Two existing Sol-medium readers inspected passages with access to the human answers; the manager checked the source quotations used below. This is unblinded development, not a new accuracy test. All original labels, packets, prompts, and responses remain unchanged. The appended generated tables separate additional model positives from missed human positives and compare the existing human coders on the same reports.

The original workbook describes character/scale/preservation as a substantive *local* issue. V3 explicitly permits substantive discussion in CPC consideration. Tyler's saved disagreement discussion acknowledges having required local concern rather than counting issues addressed through conditions without explicit opposition. In the current table, the two researchers disagree in 19 of 55 completed, jointly coded reports for each of these fields, always Jacob 1 and Tyler 0. These are field-specific counts, not a claim that the same 19 reports disagree on both. Thus a nonconflicting value from one researcher is useful evidence, but is not an adjudicated truth.

Revisions have an additional ambiguity. The codebook combines an application change with a substantive commitment or mitigation; it does not require a new concession caused by local opposition. The cases below include actual post-hearing changes, existing binding mitigation, CPC-imposed conditions, and uncertain sponsor attribution. None of the seven revision disagreements is simply a request with no documented action anywhere in the packet. Requested-versus-adopted remains an important rule, but it does not by itself explain these seven mismatches. In particular, an original mitigation may satisfy the broad legacy field while providing no evidence that local pressure changed the project.

The unrun candidate `cpc_context_rules_v4_candidate.txt`, in the pilot's code folder, keeps the existing schema and broad legacy measures. It excludes generic development potential and neutral design presentations from the character issue; it includes concrete design questions, conditions, and assessments even when the actor supports the application. It requires revision evidence to distinguish adoption, timing, actor, and focal application. It proposes excluding auction scheduling alone while retaining substantively explained development/disposition restrictions. It does not relabel the old readings or claim improved accuracy. Timing remains an explicit evidence qualification, not a new validated numeric outcome. Any later test should use other already-coded reports rather than report improved fit on these development examples as validation.

### Source checks for every disagreement

Unless stated otherwise, each listed field has human 0 and Sol 1. Page numbers refer to the supplied source PDF. These are diagnostic judgments, not replacement human labels. Short exact extracts are checked against the frozen page text; OCR spellings are preserved where quoted.

**C 930227 PPQ — character and revision.** Focal source `9c32b9eff5a407a7ce5f`, p. 3, says CPC asks DGS to auction adjacent properties together to “maximize the development potential of both properties”. That phrase does not establish a scale or character issue, so the character positive is model overreach. Joint auction is an adopted condition, however, making revision a boundary between auction administration and a substantive application change. The repeated readers split on both fields. The proposed rule excludes auction scheduling alone; the existing v3 result is retained.

**C 760132 TCM — character, human 1 and Sol 0.** Focal source `0e6500033b0a740f5091`, p. 1, records a CB request that the plan “be revised to allow direct access to the entrance of the restaurant” and occupy less sidewalk. CPC identifies obstructed access and obtains revised cafe plans. This is actual physical layout review, supporting the broad design field; it does not by itself establish neighborhood character. The likely miss exposes how the combined field's word *design* extends beyond large buildings.

**C 900211 ZSQ — character, human 1 and Sol 0.** Focal source `3d07e86dd853ab6a262c`, p. 3, records concern that losing an industrial/commercial facility “will erode the economic base of the area”, together with tax, traffic, health, and contamination concerns. Page 5 discusses compatibility of the church and nearby industrial uses. This is a genuine land-use dispute, but classifying employment and tax-base protection as physical character is uncertain. The candidate does not infer character from those economic concerns alone; their opposition remains recorded in other fields.

**C 010472 ZSX — character.** Companion source `8ef726cc2434fe58fc0f`, p. 19, explicitly evaluates the extra floor as “out-of scale with low-rise development in the surrounding community,” then finds the architectural design offsets it. The BP recommendation names the focal application along with its companions. This supports a scale issue under v3 even though the reviewer finds it mitigated. Jacob's note explicitly says he used the companion, so a missing companion cannot be assumed to explain his zero.

**C 770408 HOX — character and revision.** Focal source `b1572a478dd1161fb32e`, p. 3, says the developer “stated it would make the following changes as change orders during construction”, in a November 7 letter after the October 26 hearing. Changes include elevator access and laundry-room location. Page 4 identifies remaining defects: “Nearly half the kitchens in the project are without windows.” The revision has direct support; character depends on whether the broad design category includes interior/layout problems. The candidate includes actual reviewed layout problems in broad design, without automatically calling them neighborhood character.

**C 020048 PPK — character and revision.** The focal source `a46b9f409bc0222e209d`, p. 4, directs readers to the related special-permit report for the BP recommendation. Companion `0adeabfd64f3b46ba13e`, p. 9, includes a facade condition to “enhance the sense of place and security of the adjacent waterfront public access area.” On p. 24 the applicant representative commits to another loading bay in response to delivery-truck questions. Both fields have substantive support for the integrated redevelopment. The human evidence cites focal pp. 1–4, but this does not prove the companion was unread. Retain the source distinction and require the condition to govern the focal development.

**C 110106 ZMR — character.** Focal source `1f3e033a0e2bba2e8d99`, p. 4, introduces the height, facade, and residential-buffer discussion with “In response to questions raised at the public hearing”. Page 5 uses those features in CPC's consideration. This is a close boundary between substantive design review and dimensional clarification, not a clear false positive under v3. The candidate counts a concrete height/buffer question as scale/design while requiring additional explicit evidence for neighborhood character and no inference of opposition.

**C 190453 HAK — character.** Focal source `1a4a52796d298f4a766f`, p. 9, describes the developer's presentation: “the façade design of each development would relate to the colors and proportions of surrounding buildings.” CPC later praises the design. Without a concrete design question, concern, or condition, the cited material is a neutral/promotional project description. This is likely model overreach. Being presented at a hearing does not make every project feature a substantive issue.

**C 860553 ZMM — character and revision.** Focal source `640f1e7b77c39f922feb`, p. 3, expressly says CPC was “concerned with the visual impacts of the proposed building and any future construction on the Apollo Theatre.” Page 4 states that “a restrictive declaration has been signed by the applicant,” restricting streetwall and floor area. This directly supports both fields. Tyler's evidence-page entry covers pp. 1–2, while these passages are on pp. 3–4. That is a source-review flag, not proof of what he read or authorization to change his values.

**C 060272 ZMK — character and revision.** Focal source `821871736759d4dfcef4`, p. 5, records a CB condition “to prevent out of scale development on this site in the future.” Character has direct local evidence even though the zoning request was handled in a separate application. The remediation declaration on p. 3 was signed before certification and the later CB hearing. It supports the broad mitigation field, but does not establish that the later CB condition produced a new mitigation. The separate area-wide rezoning should not be called a revision to the focal application. Tyler's saved discussion says recommended changes were made, yet both the raw workbook row T012 and the imported values still show zero for these two fields. This is a source-version/review inconsistency, not a demonstrated import bug.

**C 820683 MMX — revision.** Focal source `d8e27c7ef8dd59143971`, p. 1, says HPD “has committed itself to relocating all of these on-site tenants.” Page 2 records SEBCO and Prospect Hospital funding commitments in response to CPC maintenance questions. The agency relocation commitment supports the broad measure, but its timing is unstated; older attached correspondence also documents pre-existing community maintenance commitments. The civic commitments must not silently be attributed to the applicant or city. This is evidence of a commitment, with uncertainty about a new review-induced concession.

**C 980703 PPX — revision.** Focal source `2618745abad44176f1b3`, p. 5, requires the seven specified lots to “be disposed of as an assemblage.” Page 4 explains the purpose as better site planning and active/passive recreation space. This is an adopted CPC disposition condition with a substantive rationale. It is distinct from the BP's transfer-to-HPD request, which was not adopted. The broad application-change field has support; the evidence should call it a CPC condition rather than an applicant concession.

The additional revision repeat, C 790338 PLQ, illustrates the same limitation. Companion `8ae8fae9301e2921c4c3`, p. 1, says “Elmcor will assist in the relocation of all legal tenants.” Elmcor is the sponsor/operator, while the formal applicant is the Community Development Agency. The packet documents an original relocation commitment; it does not establish a new concession during review. Readers differ on whether that sponsor commitment qualifies. The candidate requires its actor and timing to be stated rather than inferred.
