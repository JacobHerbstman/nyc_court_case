Sol medium transfers reasonably well to six additional reports, but the source review finds a missed civic supporter that a combined position score would hide. Three GPT-6 Sol readers complete all six packets, returning 216 detailed judgments and 51 selected evidence records. All 77 positive labels and 51 evidence records have exact source quotations. There is one unresolved detailed judgment. These checks establish that the reported quotations occur in the source; they do not establish complete extraction or correct interpretation.

The unchanged 36-field codebook is supplemented by four reading checks: revisit actors before assigning negatives, allow a clearly established undertaking to be part of the original proposal, consider every explicitly supported topic, and check an evidence record's status against its quotation. The readers receive no earlier labels or selection strata. Because these are different reports, this run cannot show that the clarifications improve performance relative to the previous prompt. It tests the clarified procedure on additional material.

Selection uses original completed human coding and a fixed hash rank. Of 340 human-coded records in the selection roster, 75 are eligible after requiring a ready current source and excluding earlier pilot/source-audit bundles. Six are selected without shared sources: New York Wheel, Central Riverdale/Spuyten Duyvil, 22-60 46th Street, Coney Island Homes II, Jamaica Hill/Hillcrest, and the C 970255 PPK disposition. The first two supply Council support/request cases, the next two civic support and opposition cases, and the final two add a long issue-positive report and an actor-negative comparison. New York Wheel has 50 retained segments and Jamaica Hill/Hillcrest has 24. Earlier bulk Jev processing and human analysis are not excluded. This is a targeted development sample, not a random or untouched holdout.

Using concern/request OR a specific obligation as an approximation to the older human issue categories, Sol matches 26/28 topic references and 11/11 usable actor-position references: 37/39 combined. Three possible comparisons are excluded because the original references conflict or use a nonstandard category. All 17 reference-positive comparisons are detected under this broad mapping. The concern-only topic mapping instead matches 25/28. Neither mapping exactly reproduces the older codebook, and agreement with coarse labels does not validate all 216 topic/stage judgments. Original Jacob, Tyler and nonconflicting references remain separate in the saved comparison.

The two broad-topic disagreements are both in C 970255 PPK. Jacob's original character and environmental codes are zero. The supplied Borough President recommendation, pages 13--15, requests accessory/extension/enlargement use restrictions and removal of abandoned vehicles, litter and debris. The final CPC resolution, page 9, imposes specific sale-use restrictions on four lots; it does not impose all requested cleanup conditions. Sol finds these passages and preserves that distinction. The remaining question is whether these parcel-use restrictions and cleanup requests meet the intended character and environmental issue definitions, not whether the quoted events occurred. No original human value is revised. In Jamaica Hill/Hillcrest, page 15 specifies parcel-level environmental testing and remediation; this explains why the broader mapping agrees with the human environmental positive while the concern-only mapping does not.

The important missed positive is outside the scored reference. New York Wheel, C 150447 ZSR, page 28 reports explicit support and a request for prompt approval from the Building and Construction Trades Council of Greater New York. Pages 29--30 report opposition and requests from the St. George Civic Association. Sol finds the latter but codes civic support zero. The source supports Tyler's original `both` reading: one independent organization supports and another opposes. That nonstandard human category remains visible but excluded from the prespecified score. Even a score that prioritized opposition would miss this extraction error. A separate complete actor list, with each organization's role, support, opposition and requests, is therefore a more useful next refinement than broader rewording of the topic questions. It must also retain officials' offices separately from organizations with “Council” in their name.

Other actor checks are encouraging. Sol identifies the District 11 member's support in the Riverdale report and the District 49 member's office supporting the Wheel while requesting restoration of amenities. It separates the Greek Cultural Center's support from the 22-60 46th Street applicants, and records the Coney Island Board of Trade's opposition to the residential frontage. It does not convert generic “local elected officials” in Jamaica Hill/Hillcrest into named Council members. These examples strengthen confidence in the method, but there are still only two reference-positive Council cases and no fresh Council-opposition example in this selection.

Two implementation limits remain. First, the twelve-record cap makes supplementary evidence a selection, not an exhaustive event inventory; a complete actor inventory should not share that cap. Second, four returned `source_scope` reasons describe substantive concerns rather than whether the text belongs to the focal application. The reader instruction named that flag without defining its intended meaning clearly enough. The raw flag is preserved, is not counted as a detected source-attribution error, and is never used to exclude a report. Before reuse, define it as uncertainty about application scope and give substantive concerns their existing topic fields. The single unresolved detailed judgment concerns whether the Wheel's abstract-lighting limitation establishes a historic-preservation undertaking.

The recommendation from this six-report test is to retain Sol medium and add the actor-inventory step before a larger run. This six-report test makes no API calls, changes no production labels, and leaves Jev and bulk processing paused. Sources, prompts, the selection design, original human snapshot, dispatch messages and returned readings are preserved under `data_raw/cpc_subagent_pilot/20260922_sol_validation/`. Analysis is reproduced from saved answers; a future model reading is not deterministic and per-run dollar cost is not measured. Run `make` in the existing pilot task's `code/` directory, then `make output/2026-09-22-sol-human-validation.pdf` from `logbook/`. The earlier Sol/Luna outputs remain unchanged.

## Follow-up: keep the actor reading small

Jacob approved the actor inventory but asked not to overwhelm the reader. Two fresh Sol medium readers therefore receive one report each and a 272-word actor-only instruction, including its output example. They do not receive the topic questions or earlier answers. New York Wheel and the shorter Riverdale report are deliberately revisited, with all 50 and 8 source segments unchanged. There are no repeat calls. This isolates a practical way to obtain the missed information, but two known reports and stochastic model readings cannot establish that the prompt change causes better performance generally.

The separate pass recovers the Trades Council's support while retaining St. George Civic Association's opposition and the District 49 member's office's support, concerns and amenity requests. It correctly describes the Trades Council's request as approval without delay; the civic association instead requests parking relocation, bus-access changes and garage ventilation. These request objects stay in the evidence rather than being collapsed into a common substantive-change flag. In Riverdale, the District 11 member supports the application and no independent civic organization is extracted. Support and opposition are aggregated separately, giving eight matches on eight source checks frozen before dispatch, compared with seven for the earlier v2 labels. These are checks on known cases, not an estimate of population accuracy.

The inventories contain 19 actor/office records and 44 statements, with exact source quotations for all 44. Both readers report reading every segment. The inventory also preserves uncertainty: the Riverdale report's generic community is not turned into a named civic organization, and the Wheel's technical consultant has an unclear role. Exact quotes do not establish perfect interpretation. For example, the Wheel inventory records CPC's acknowledgment of residents' concerns as a CPC concern; the source wording establishes acknowledgment, not necessarily CPC's own objection. This does not affect the Council/civic support and opposition checks, but it cautions against treating every concern record as the actor's personal objection. The raw reading is retained without correction, and a complete independent actor census remains unavailable.

The narrow pass is a useful addition before further small validation. The topic questions and all prior labels remain unchanged; hashes match for all 191 prior Sol, Luna and Jev pilot outputs checked. Sources, short prompt, pre-dispatch checks, dispatch and raw readings are preserved under `data_raw/cpc_subagent_pilot/20260922_sol_actors/`. The existing pilot task now produces a statement inventory and paired comparison from these saved readings, linked into this logbook through Make. Jev and bulk processing remain paused.

## Follow-up: six additional actor readings

The same short actor prompt works well for the two selected Council-opposition cases, but the broader inventory still needs a few precise definitions. Three fresh Sol medium readers process six additional reports, two each, returning 63 actor/office records and 99 statements with exact source quotations. Every report declares all supplied segments read. The topic questions are not asked again. A fixed hash rank chooses DUMBO, Parsons Boulevard and Flatbush/Caton outside the older pilot/source-audit sources, plus 610 Lexington, C7 Baychester and Bartow animal shelter as previously studied stress cases. There were no Council-positive cases left under the stricter unused-source exclusions. Earlier bulk Jev processing and human coding are not excluded; this is targeted development, not a representative holdout.

All six Council categories agree with available original coding, including explicit opposition at 610 Lexington and C7 Baychester. Across the three less-studied reports, broad actor categories match Jacob on 4/4 comparisons and Tyler on 3/4; the three older stress cases match Jacob on 3/4 and Tyler on 2/4. These sixteen coder-specific comparisons include overlapping judgments and are not sixteen independent reports. All four mismatches concern civic categories. DUMBO clearly supports the model's additional positive: three named organizations support while the DUMBO Neighborhood Alliance opposes. At 610 Lexington, the inventory captures block associations' concerns without asserting an explicit opposing stance. In Baychester, the opponent is both a resident and a Riverbay board member; a single role places the opposition under residents despite preserving the affiliation in the name. At Bartow, the inventory captures opposing residents separately from supporting organizations. Those last three differences reveal definition or attribution questions; they do not establish that either coder is wrong.

Source review identifies concrete inventory problems outside those coarse scores. Bartow's Animal Care Centers is the proposed facility's operator and occupant, so its nonprofit status does not make it an independent civic supporter. Unnamed advocates submitting signatures also do not establish an organization. Both exclusions were explicit in the longer existing codebook but were lost when the actor instruction was shortened. Bartow's Community Board formally recommends approval only if the shelter is placed at a different site: that rejects this submitted site, as Jacob's existing note already records, while Sol calls it support plus a relocation request. Finally, the Flatbush/Caton companion report records an unidentified opposing speaker at the joint hearing, omitted from the inventory. The speaker's identity remains unknown and the two reports' hearing counts differ; retain that evidence with its own source application, without inventing a civic affiliation or adding counts.

These results support keeping Sol medium and the separate actor pass, while restoring three short distinctions before a larger run: organizations must be independent of the project team and actually identified as organizations; agreement only at another site is not support for this site; and relevant paired-report testimony must remain visible with its source application. Council-member roles are working better than civic affiliation in this test. The successful Parsons Boulevard reading also preserves the failure to adopt the favorable draft CPC report, showing why full-report context matters. None of these observations is used to edit the raw readings or original human codes. Nine post-reading manager source observations are saved separately, with quoted passages and explicit reasons; they are not independent gold labels.

The selection, unchanged prompt, original references, dispatch and raw answers are frozen in the archive for this test, identified in the pilot task README. The existing selection and actor-comparison scripts now accept a scalar version argument, preserving the older outputs byte for byte; the 194 prior pilot outputs checked remain unchanged. Normal Make reproduces the saved-answer comparison and source-review table without model calls. No repeated inference, topic recoding or corpus-scale processing was performed. The same logbook Make target now includes the new findings.
