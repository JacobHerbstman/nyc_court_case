Jacob approved processing the full narrative corpus and auditing the results afterward, accepting that some errors will remain and that a later revised pass may be useful. The first full pass retains 8,905 narratives assembled from the existing CPC corpus. The source coverage table retains all 11,314 source records, including unavailable text, companion reports, and narratives represented by another report. A separate coverage table retains all 32,964 ZAP projects. A withdrawn project without a report remains observable as a project with no extractable CPC narrative; it is never treated as having no opposition or no concerns.

The frozen questions distinguish discussion, concern or request, and an adopted commitment for eight topics. Five Council questions distinguish support, opposition, requests for a project, requests for a provision, and a motivating concern. Three civic questions distinguish support, opposition, and requests. Jacob's decision that a Council member's concerns prompting a rezoning count as involvement is included. These are 32 substantive fields, accompanied by 16 independent choices of supporting text for the yes/no concern and actor questions. Adoption questions also select supporting text. The original Jacob and Tyler labels remain intact.

This pass preserves the wording used in the topic and Council pilots. General adoption questions come from the earlier topic pilot and have weaker validation than the refined Council questions. They identify an accepted undertaking or binding substantive condition, not necessarily a revision after certification or a response caused by opposition. Other original fields, including numerical vote counts and the broad revision indicator, are not newly measured by this question set. Those existing data are not overwritten.

The initial full-corpus packet format exceeded the provider's token limit on a long report. That first vintage is preserved: three reports succeeded, six requests returned server errors, and one returned a token-limit error. The corrected packet format uses short segment identifiers and at most 45,000 state characters and 20 text segments per request. It retains every segment, producing 14,186 planned requests; 2,230 narratives require multiple parts. The three fully completed original reports are carried forward without another inference call. The wording did not change between packet vintages. Split context remains an explicit limitation because a qualification on another part may affect a judgment.

Acquisition records exact requests, raw responses, source hashes, source pages and offsets, HTTP outcomes, and account-credit checks. The runner uses two workers with starts spaced by at least five seconds after an initial provider rate limit. It stops on a new rate limit, repeated failures, an unexpected response, or observed account charges. An explicit resume skips all previously started requests, including unresolved outcomes, so a technical failure remains visible for a separately documented recovery pass. Normal Make builds never call the API. The account usage increase has been zero at the saved checks; the generated snapshot below reports the latest observation rather than assuming promotional pricing will persist.

Before observing corpus answers, 24 reports were selected randomly from the entire narrative roster using a fixed hash seed. Three Sol-medium readers each read eight complete source packets without access to Jev answers or old human coding. Their 768 judgments and exact supporting quotations are archived. These are independent AI judgments, not a human gold standard. The audit also compares the available results with all 340 previously coded reports and preserves missing results in the denominators. A separate targeted queue identifies evidence conflicts and positive actor or topic findings for later source review. Its contents do not enter the random-audit rates.

The numerical appendix is a snapshot of whatever responses have been saved when the report is built. A running or stopped acquisition can therefore yield an incomplete report. Negative labels require every relevant part to answer negatively; any positive part yields a provisional positive. Failed calls and exact probability ties stay missing unless another part supplies a positive. Neither successful extraction nor agreement with a second model establishes that the source universe or the substantive labels are error-free.

An initial source check of three audit disagreements found substantive boundaries worth retaining for later review. In C 160378 ZMR, Jev selected the air-quality stack restriction on page 3 as evidence of adopted character and design commitments; the source specifically describes environmental mitigation, while page 5 describes otherwise unchanged zoning requirements. In C 780600 HDM, Jev treated a community sponsor listed on page 1 as civic support, although the report records no attributed statement of support and no appearances at the CPC hearing. In C 050032 HAM, page 5 records the Community Board's request for a written lifetime affordability commitment and refers to earlier income and rent commitments; it does not establish that the requested lifetime extension was accepted. The last case is especially sensitive to the distinction between an existing promise and acceptance of the Board's additional request. These checks do not alter either model's archived answer or establish an overall error rate.

Reproduction: on branch `cpc_llm_training`, starting from revision `6077733`, the new production task is `tasks/extract_ulurp_cpc_jev_labels`. Run `make acquire` from its `code/` directory only when intentionally acquiring unattempted public reports. Run `make output/2026-09-19-jev-corpus.pdf` from `logbook/` to rebuild saved-response labels, source and ZAP coverage, audit comparisons, and this report through declared file dependencies. Source and response vintages are dated September 19, 2026; acquisition timestamps use UTC and can fall on September 20 or later.

**September 20 acquisition update.** The overnight run stopped at 03:27:58 UTC on a temporary provider rate limit requesting a two-second wait. At that point 731 narratives were complete, 70 partial, 96 failed, and 8,008 unattempted. Account usage remained zero with five dollars available. The runner had treated every rate limit as a terminal stop. It now waits at least 60 seconds, respects a longer provider Retry-After interval, and increases waits for successive rate limits before continuing to unattempted reports. Six limits without a successful response still stop the run for inspection. Existing answers and failed attempts remain unchanged. Local fixtures verified cooldown timing, continued progress, no duplicate requests, persistence of in-flight outcomes, and stops on persistent limits, invalid answers, or observed charges. The remaining first-pass work was resumed on September 20; question wording and source packets were unchanged.

**September 20 usable-sample decision.** Jacob requested that we keep only the reports that worked and continue processing. The working dataset, `cpc_jev_successful_labels_v2.csv`, now includes a report only if every requested part returned a valid response. Agreement comparisons and the targeted review queue use this same sample. Reports with even one failed or unfinished part are excluded from working results, while the complete case roster, source links, raw answers, and missingness reasons remain available for coverage checks. This is a technical completion restriction, not a claim that the surviving judgments are correct. Exact probability ties remain missing even in successfully processed reports, and all 24 random-audit reports and 340 previously coded reports remain in the reference tables.

**September 20 provider outage.** The resumed batch stopped at 18:13 UTC after ten consecutive HTTP 503 service-unavailable responses. At that point 944 narratives were complete, 152 partial, 276 failed, and 7,533 unattempted; account usage remained zero. On Jacob's instruction to continue, the acquisition pace was reduced to one worker with at least ten seconds between starts. A run of ten transient failures now triggers a 15-minute cooldown; continued failures trigger 30- and 60-minute cooldowns before another failure ends the run. A successful response resets that sequence. These waits do not repeat a completed or failed request, change the questions, or admit unsuccessful reports into the working sample.

**September 20 failure recovery.** After reviewing the error responses, Jacob asked us to keep trying. Recovery is now authorized for calls that returned temporary service errors: HTTP 429, 502, 503, 504, or 529. Two recovery passes are queued after the active first pass, with a 15-minute wait before each and the same slower request pace and outage cooldowns. Successfully answered requests are excluded. Each retry uses the identical frozen body and appends an explicit attempt number; earlier records remain unchanged. The first valid response is retained, and no second judgment is requested after success. Invalid answers and outcomes without a recorded server response remain unresolved. This change can recover reports missing a single failed part while retaining the requirement that all parts succeed before a report enters the working dataset. Recovery results are not yet claimed merely because the passes are queued.

**September 21 acquisition update.** At 18:56 UTC, 2,518 of 8,905 narratives were complete, 196 partial, 213 attempted without a successful part, and 5,978 unattempted. Recovery had obtained 951 successful request parts, not necessarily 951 complete reports. Account usage remained zero. The first pass had stopped after a connection reset during a credit check; its responses were saved and no started request lacked a recorded outcome. Recovery subsequently stopped on one nonretryable token-limit error for `b5cd848301e1fc22eabd_part003`. This is a packet-size failure, unlike the earlier temporary service errors; its report remains incomplete and needs a separately documented smaller-packet remedy. The remaining unattempted requests were resumed, with unfinished eligible recovery attempts queued afterward. Successful answers, frozen packets, questions, and the maximum of three attempts remain unchanged.

**September 21 reliability and pace.** The acquisition now retries temporary failures of the read-only credit check before submitting any further model requests. A known token-limit rejection is retained for repair without stopping unrelated reports. Extended service outages trigger waits of 15, 30, then 60 minutes, continuing at hourly intervals until a response succeeds; restarting preserves the remaining cooldown. Charges, authentication failures, invalid answers, persistent credit-check failures, and repeated rate limits still stop the batch. The failure-recovery ceiling is now six total attempts per request, always excluding successful answers. Local fixtures verified those boundaries, preservation of frozen requests and earlier observations, recovery on the sixth attempt, and exclusion of incomplete reports from the working dataset.

A bounded faster-pace trial recorded 72 previously unattempted requests, with 26 valid responses and 46 service-unavailable errors over approximately 3.6 minutes. Ten consecutive errors triggered a 15-minute cooldown. This short, different-request sample does not identify the effect of request speed on failures or establish a completion forecast. Jacob then clarified that steady progress mattered more than a next-day deadline. The trial was ended during its cooldown, its observations were preserved, and the full run returned to one worker with ten-second spacing, honoring the remaining wait. A proposed recurring scheduled check was rejected by automatic approval review because unattended API restarts needed explicit authorization; no such automation was created.

**September 21 quality pause.** After the mid-run audit demonstrated civic opposition from a Brooklyn disposition being assigned to a separate Queens application, Jacob requested that Jev stop while source attribution is repaired. Both the first-pass process and the queued recovery processes were terminated, and the acquisition entry point now refuses new API calls. The saved journal has 6,861 started attempts and exactly 6,861 recorded outcomes, with no unmatched starts. The last account observation remains zero dollars used and five dollars available. All earlier requests and responses are preserved. Normal builds use those saved observations only. This pause supersedes the earlier instructions to continue or retry; no restart is authorized while the source-matching problem remains unresolved.
