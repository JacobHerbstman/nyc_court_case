Jacob confirmed that a Council member's concerns prompting a rezoning count as
a fair yes for substantive involvement. Sunset Park therefore remains a positive
case. The literal v9/v10 question about an explicit request is a narrower
diagnostic, not a failure to identify involvement. We preserve its original
expected answer and raw result. For the new v11 comparison, any of the five
signals establishes involvement; motivating concerns also enter the broad legacy
support/request category when opposition is negative. Explicit endorsement remains
its own question and cannot be inferred from that broad category.

The larger test freezes the five best Council questions without further wording
changes and applies them to fifty other already-coded reports, spanning 1977--2025.
It includes all ten eligible original Council-positive bundles and forty seeded
original negatives, with 790 pages from seventy public NYC PDFs. Original labels
choose and evaluate the sample but are never sent to Jev. All 340 coded documents
remain in the roster: 125 overlap earlier pilots or source reviews, 25 exceed
the complete-text limit, 18 lack a complete nonconflicting original Council
reference, and 122 are outside this batch or overlap its selected sources.
These are restrictions on this test only. Long reports, uncoded reports and
withdrawn applications remain in the research universe.

All fifty calls were attempted once: 47 succeeded, two returned HTTP 503 and
one returned HTTP 504. The three unavailable reports remain missing. Among the
47 answered reports, broad involvement agrees with the original coding in 46:
nine of ten original positives are found and all 37 available original negatives
remain negative. The finer opposition-versus-support/request category agrees in
45 of 47. There are no exact probability ties; six binary/page pairs conflict.
The observed account usage increase is $0 and the balance remains $5, with
516,977 reported input tokens and 107,293 output tokens. These findings support
a broader Council pass with flags retained, rather than treating the candidates
as final validated labels. The smaller topic and civic experiments do not acquire
this validation merely because the Council questions transferred well.

The binary questions retain endorsement, opposition/withdrawal, project requests,
requests for particular provisions, and concerns that prompted the proposal.
Five independent page selectors record candidate supporting evidence. Their
answers do not overwrite binary judgments. One call is allowed per new report;
rate limits and observed account charges stop acquisition. The results below
are compared with imperfect original codes, not assumed human truth. They do
not estimate corpus prevalence because selection prioritizes positives.

Two comparisons illustrate why disagreement with the old coding is not always
a model error. In `C 190403 ZMX`, page 23 reports the member's concerns about
DOB enforcement but also support for several aspects of the proposed text.
Jacob's original code is opposition; Jev detects involvement and support plus a
request, with opposition below the cutoff. The source presents mixed views,
so this is a substantive coding distinction rather than a missed Council actor.
Tyler's positive code for `C 180042 ZMK` is supported on page 13, where the
applicant intends to collaborate with nonprofit groups suggested by the local
Council member. Jev selects that very page for a provision request, but its
binary score is 0.49 and the resulting broad involvement code is negative.
This is a plausible missed recommendation under our broad definition. The
independent page/binary conflict marks it for inspection without changing the
raw answer or the 0.5 threshold. No call was repeated to repair either case.

## Earlier diagnostic evidence

Jacob prioritized Council-member coding after v8 missed all three available
positive cases. Two bounded refinements recover substantive Council involvement
in those three reports and three additional coded reports, while retaining
negative answers for three controls. The current full-report results match
44 of 45 component expectations. This is a selected nine-report diagnostic,
not an accuracy estimate for the corpus. One component differs under the narrower
explicit-request definition, and short excerpts retain two other errors. Original
human codes, earlier results and the frozen acceptance/timing wording are unchanged.

The key change is to preserve five distinct signals: explicit endorsement,
opposition or a withdrawal request, a request to create the project, a request
for a particular provision, and concerns that helped prompt the proposal.
Representatives explicitly speaking for a member or office count; a member's
personal name is unnecessary. Institutional Council actions, routine filing,
other elected officials and unrelated projects do not establish an individual
member's position. Any positive signal establishes substantive involvement.
It does not automatically establish endorsement.

The source distinctions are concrete:

- `C 870650 ZSQ` has a companion, `C 870605 PSQ`, whose page 7 lists a
  representative of the Councilman from the 15th district among opponents.
  The revised full-report opposition probability is 0.57, versus 0.22 in v8.
  The excerpt probability is 0.94. The full result is above our fixed threshold
  but is not a calibrated measure of confidence in this coding.
- `C 970324 DMM`, page 12, says Council Member Linares requested development
  of the garage. The final full-report project-request probability is 0.77;
  endorsement is 0.34. The request is recovered without relabeling it support.
- `C 090387 ZMK`, page 22, attributes the rezoning to concerns from the local
  Council member, Community Board and residents. Motivating concern is 0.86.
  The project-request answer remains slightly positive at 0.53 despite our
  frozen expectation of insufficient evidence for that stronger claim.
  Council involvement is established and Jacob accepts it as a fair positive.
- In another coded report, `C 210174 ZMK`, page 7 attributes selection of
  MIH Option 2 to recommendations by the local Council member and Community
  Board. The first revision missed this recommendation at 0.36. Asking whether
  the member recommended a particular feature recovers it at 0.72 on the full
  report and 0.92 on the page excerpt. Endorsement stays negative.
- `C 760072 HDK`, pages 3--4, separately records Council representation among
  supporters and a request for Section 8 subsidies. Both are recovered, at
  0.95 and 0.84. `C 890619 PSK`, page 10, explicitly requests withdrawal of
  the application; opposition is correctly positive at 0.96.

The three controls are `C 090367 ZSM` (routine filing and institutional powers),
`C 180085 ZMQ` (an earlier Council rezoning action and a filing notice), and
`C 760218 MMQ` (Community Board and Board of Estimate actions). All remain
negative for substantive individual Council involvement. The additional positive
reports were selected using original human codes and source inspection before
calls. They were not an untouched random validation set. Expected components
are Codex's recorded source judgments, not new human adjudications.

The first refinement, v9, submitted twelve distinct requests: nine full bundles
and three excerpts of earlier misses. It recovered all three original misses,
but missed the additional affordability recommendation and differed from the
literal project-request expectation in Sunset Park. The narrower v10 follow-up changed
only endorsement and the two request questions, with six full reports and three
excerpt controls. It submitted no unchanged opposition or motivating-concern
questions. It recovered the affordability recommendation, improved the garage
project-request score and reduced the Sunset Park project-request probability from 0.78 to
0.53, but did not eliminate it. The threshold remains 0.5; no answer was tuned
or discarded to make the test appear perfect.

Short excerpts still overread the garage request as endorsement (0.53) and as a
request for a particular provision (0.61). Those two errors disappear in the
full report. Conversely, full context gives weaker detection than the excerpt
for the Rockaway opposition. We should retain complete reports and source
passages; these results do not support a blanket rule that shorter or longer
context always performs better. The nine-report combined dataset carries a
field naming every diagnostic disagreement, including Sunset Park's project
request. Its raw candidate is preserved and is not a validated production code.

The combined diagnostic dataset contains 18 revised v10 answers and 27 carried
v9 answers, with origins recorded. These are not 45 independent new tests.
The v10 calls alone match 17/18 full-report expectations and 7/9 excerpt
expectations; the combined readings detect all six known involvement cases.

All 21 distinct requests succeeded at an observed account charge of $0; the
balance remains $5. Reported usage totals 149,917 input and 1,644 output tokens.
Calls were paced ten seconds apart, with one attempt per request and rate/charge
stops. Previous codebooks, results and archives retain their bytes.
