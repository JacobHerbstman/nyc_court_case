The next useful CPC dataset should distinguish a reported vote from an interpretation of what that vote means. Jacob asked to combine Tyler's explicit vote and evidence fields with our related-document handling, extend reliable regex extraction, and reserve contextual judgments for later AI reading. The new fields preserve the reported tally, proposal-aligned tally, formal recommendation, abstentions, rule, evidence, and source report separately. The appended coverage results measure how often these rules produce a resolved record. They do not establish a certified accuracy rate.

South Avenue (C 160174 ZSR) illustrates why this separation matters. Its report gives 17 affirmative votes, 14 negative votes, and five abstentions, explicitly states that abstentions count as disapproval, and reports a denied recommendation to approve. We retain 17/14/5 as the literal counts and record effective opposition of 19. This is a report-specific rule, not a decision to recode every abstention as opposition. A different example, C 780349 TCM, reports 21 affirmative votes and two negative votes on a disapproval motion; the proposal-aligned counts are two for and 21 against. Contradictory vote orientation, multiple speaker groups, multiple boards or tallies, conditions rejecting the proposed site, and continued hearings require review rather than automatic alignment or aggregation.

The counting rules now recognize a wider range of written numbers, targeted OCR spacing, explicit resolution language, and speaker descriptions between the quantity and the stance. Bounded prose transitions recover some older sections that cross printed lines. A companion report may supply absent evidence, but cannot override a contradictory focal recommendation; resolved companions must agree on the count record, including the abstention rule. The selected narrative sample and source text hashes are retained. The revised counts and formal positions change 449 CB-opposition flags, 324 substantial-opposition flags, and 127 approved-with-unresolved-objection flags. These derived indicators remain contextual proxies and are not covered by the resolved-count claim; the other prose rules were not rewritten. Count plots use only resolved records; partial or ambiguous tallies remain available in the CSV with their status. These CPC narratives remain a document sample linked to the full ZAP universe. Withdrawn and terminated projects without CPC text must remain in analyses of project progression.

The comparison with Jacob's existing 200 reports is developmental. The appendix also reports agreement with both Codex-coded benchmarks so that the human calibration result is not presented alone. Both 100-report Codex samples have now been inspected and are regression benchmarks, including the one whose stable filename still says `holdout`. A new 150-report review sheet excludes earlier coded reports and additional reports inspected while developing the current rules. It balances decade, action family, and extraction route, includes sampling weights, and omits predictions. Independent human coding is the next step before declaring a fraction of the corpus reliable. Tyler's parser and row-level output are still unavailable; this implementation adopts features described in his PDF and does not claim a measured head-to-head result against his code.

The accompanying cleanup follows the local aldermanic_privilege, hclose_current-master, and gottlieb_work examples and the current readable-research-code and research-workflow skills. Concrete source paths replace manifest lookups in the homeownership measure and selected audits. The main and audit Makefiles share one short upstream rule and use plain input symlinks. Production scripts with coupled outputs have real producer rules instead of secondary existence checks; committed manual coding has no invented producer. Four paper tasks use a small shared CSV/report writer. The counting rules are shared with their tests, the previous one-off comparison script is removed, and its results are frozen at commit 94bd806 in the earlier logbook appendix. No new task or build framework is introduced.

The cleanup preserves the paper's 59-row homeownership measure, 41,064-row panel, 22 event-study rows, and eight long-difference rows exactly as parsed CSV values. The paper builds through Make. All 27 other changed primary task Makefiles built successfully and their subsequent dry runs proposed no producer or linking commands. Checks exercise unchanged builds, changed-input propagation in a disposable Make fixture, regeneration of a missing actual estimate output, report creation and key assertions, and source download failure/preservation. The acquisition helper now fails without deleting a saved snapshot or replacing it with different bytes under the old vintage. These checks concern reproducibility and extraction behavior, not causal identification.

The repository is not yet fully replicable across historical auxiliary analyses. The broader Make review finds consumers of removed 1980 NHGIS and Council outputs, old corpus PDF filenames, and an archived boundary task with a stale include path. Two empty directories had previously been created under filenames expected to be data; those misleading artifacts were removed. The exact-decadal validation and 2010 Council-district homeownership measure now build using explicit source files, but the baseline-control audit still requires restoration of its 1980 producer. These failures are outside the verified paper and current CPC extraction path and should be resolved before reviving those analyses.
