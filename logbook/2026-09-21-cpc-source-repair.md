The mid-run audit found separate C-O-P dispositions bundled by a generic title and decision date. Jev consequently assigned Brooklyn civic opposition to an unrelated Queens application. The repair removes title/date-only links at the source producer. Shared ZAP IDs on the same date, explicit same-date application references, identical normalized narratives and recorded companion decisions still establish relationships. Designated-lead substitution now requires the existing name/date match plus the same nonempty ZAP ID set. Every context link records its relationship path and basis.

The corrected roster contains 9,063 narratives: all 8,905 previous narratives plus 158 no longer folded into a designated lead without matching ZAP identity. The complete source manifest and all 7,033 original human/reconciled coding records are unchanged. The selection audit reconstructs narrative hashes and verifies preservation of represented applications, exact duplicates and the previously verified N companions. These checks establish preservation under the repair, not complete historical archive coverage.

Old and new inputs are separate. The paused v2 run retains its requests, responses, segments, roster, source links, manifest, human-reference snapshot and preparation code. Historical builds use that snapshot. The 30-report subagent pilot reproduces its saved request hashes; its human table is the original columns projected from the frozen reconciliation. Corrected packets are v3, with unchanged questions so source changes can be assessed separately. An identical successful request can be reused, but identical input does not establish a correct answer.

Each PDF page now receives an application-scope status. Main-report pages use the parsed CPC boundary; attachments need a docket identity or a recorded source-specific decision. Docket lines may omit C/N, but incidental prose citations and OCR-corrupted identifiers do not establish identity. Unanchored attachments, conflicting boroughs and unclear boundaries remain unresolved. Known unrelated pages are excluded through hash-locked source reviews. Identified continuations retain their block identity. Distinct full PDFs remain available even when their narrative prefixes repeat. Low-text pages are flagged; scope checks do not establish OCR completeness.

Any report with unresolved page scope remains on the roster but outside the prepared request queue. No missing passage becomes a negative answer. Queens C 920197 PPQ retains pages 1–6 and excludes unrelated Manhattan material on pages 7–11. C 910119 HUK retains Councilmember Wooten's opposition through its genuine C 920049 HDK companion while excluding its appended Saratoga Square statement. Reviewed Bay Ridge attachments remain available. An unreadable Brownsville map remains unresolved. The numerical appendix reports readiness and potential reuse.

The source-repair phase prepared a separate, initially unrun candidate codebook that adds review-stage Council concerns, distinguishes residents from civic organizations, and excludes generic plan-conformity clauses from substantive adopted commitments. All topic-discussion and topic-concern questions remain unchanged. A bounded validation should recover the Council concern in C 230091 ZMQ, page 8; preserve ASPCA support while rejecting resident opposition as civic opposition in C 180346 PSX, pages 5–6; and reject generic plan compliance as a scale/design commitment in C 010691 ZSM, page 6. Fresh reports and previously supported commitments are also needed: these known-error checks are development cases, not an accuracy estimate.

Bulk acquisition remains paused; the source-repair phase made no API calls. The subsequent bounded validation is recorded in the September 21 Jev repair-test entry. Attachment boundaries and question validation remain unresolved before bulk resumption. Ordinary Make builds use saved sources only. Build the narrative, extraction and audit tasks from their code directories, then this report through the logbook Makefile. Multiple-output rules are confined to the local output directory, preventing historical pilot filenames from intercepting upstream corpus targets. The current audit is the default; historical targets remain available explicitly.
