The partially completed corpus contains useful signals, but the audit found a source-assignment problem that should be fixed before using the labels for analysis. A Queens disposition, C 920197 PPQ, inherits civic opposition from a separate Brooklyn disposition included as context. Both share the generic title C-O-P and a vote date. The source-link code connects such groups without requiring a shared ZAP project. Full PDFs can also contain appended testimony about unrelated applications: the Queens PDF has Manhattan testimony after its own recommendations. Keeping these sources is appropriate; attributing their contents to the wrong project is not.

Council positions look more promising than civic positions or the combined character measure in the existing coding comparison. The human benchmark is imperfect and was used during question development, so its agreement rates are diagnostics, not estimates of accuracy on all reports. The original random source audit and twelve new positive-case readings provide complementary checks; the new readings deliberately overrepresent positives and are also AI judgments. A separate source-link screen identifies candidates for checking, not an established count of contaminated reports.

Two distinctions matter for use of these data. First, a topic concern and an adopted commitment are different events. Combining them increases disagreement with older concern coding, especially for character, scale and preservation. Reading sources confirms that a routine requirement to follow approved plans can receive a positive design-commitment label. Some other apparent false positives are real accepted restrictions whose topic classification is debatable. The concern-only fields reduce this problem but still miss some concerns and add others. The adoption fields do not provide a validated measure of a proposal changing after certification or changing because of opposition.

Second, actors need their own checks. Jev correctly locates Councilmember Wooten's opposition in a related report, while the older reference records support/request. It also mistakes residents' opposition to a Bronx animal shelter for civic-group opposition, and misses a named homeowners association's support in another report. Some older civic disagreements are definitional: the frozen questions allow an unnamed community group but exclude an organization that is itself the applicant. Those rules differ from some original coding.

The Council questions also have a coverage gap. A member can raise a concern during review without endorsing or opposing the whole proposal, explicitly requesting a provision, or having concerns that originally prompted the proposal. C 230091 ZMQ contains such a concern about recreational use. The union of the five frozen questions should therefore be described as the five captured Council signals, not proof of all Council involvement.

The first repair priority is application attribution: verify companion relationships and separate unrelated attachments while retaining every original report. Next, refine routine approval language, actor attribution, and Council concerns raised during review. Evaluate corrections on additional source cases before replacing any existing answers. Neither current character/adoption nor civic fields are ready for substantive conclusions. Existing human and model labels remain unchanged. Saved source passages and request-level answers let us identify affected cases and target corrections, although some requests will need new readings.
