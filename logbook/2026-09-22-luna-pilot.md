title: "Comparing complete CPC readings with Luna and Sol"
date: "September 22, 2026"
---

GPT-6 Luna can complete the reading task, including a long report and its declaration, and merits a larger controlled test. Three Luna subagents at medium reasoning returned all four assigned report packets, 144 field judgments and 31 supplementary evidence records. All 50 positive labels and all 31 evidence records have quotations found verbatim in their cited source segments. This verifies the quotations' provenance, not the correctness of the interpretations. The readers also report reading every supplied segment; that declaration is not independent proof of complete attention.

Against 79 available earlier reference decisions, Luna agrees on 75, capturing 29 of 33 reference positives and all 46 reference negatives. These references are fallible earlier Sol-medium or manager source readings, not a human gold standard. They are concentrated in Commerce Avenue and the ASPCA site, which contribute 36 fields each; Sunset Park contributes four and Whitestone Lanes three. The 65 remaining Luna fields lack a reference. One of these is unresolved. Thus 75/79 is agreement on selected decisions, not a population accuracy estimate. It also has a different unit and denominator from the earlier 14/20 passage-check result.

The paired comparison offers a modest improvement over the revised Jev procedure where both returned results: Luna matches 33 of the same 36 reference decisions, versus 32 for revised Jev. Against the older Jev procedure's 40 available decisions, Luna matches 40 and Jev 31. These are comparisons of entire reading procedures: question wording, whole-report context, evidence retrieval and verification differ. The older adopted-change questions are broader than the current specific-obligation definition. Better availability is a clear practical advantage in this trial, but four deliberately selected difficult reports do not establish general model superiority. Neither reader cost nor latency was experimentally compared.

Source inspection confirms two clear misses. Commerce Avenue, C 190426 PCX, page 5 explicitly says the Commission asked whether the smaller temporary sanitation facility could maintain service capacity. Luna codes no infrastructure/service concern. Whitestone Lanes, C 230091 ZMQ, page 8 explicitly says the president of the Linden Towers Co-Op Homeowners Association spoke in favor. Luna codes no civic support, incorrectly claiming support came only from public bodies and project speakers. Its Council fields match all 13 available references, but only one is positive; civic fields capture only one of two reference positives. These results do not yet validate Council or civic coding.

The other two disagreements concern the Commerce Avenue tree buffer and street trees. Page 5 records definite applicant statements that trees will be planted as a buffer and provided on the street frontages. Luna recognizes tree planting as an applicant promise in its supplementary evidence, but tags it only as environmental and returns no character or design obligation. The earlier reference gives both topics a positive. The buffer's purpose supports compatibility coding; whether every frontage planting also qualifies as design needs an explicit topic rule. This is principally a topic-mapping issue, rather than a failure to find the passage or distinguish a request from a promise. Luna correctly leaves the community board's requested parking conditions as requests; the final CPC approval does not adopt them.

The full sources also expose a reference-scope problem. Sunset Park Group III, C 800193 HPK, is bundled with Groups II and IV, C 800192 and C 800194 HPK. The current links retain these as context through explicit application references and a shared vote date; the three records have distinct ZAP project identifiers. They are related parts of a larger program, not interchangeable projects. Luna flags the distinction and restricts focal labels to Group III. The earlier fixed passage audit used Group IV's statement that a new structure was brought to the Fourth Avenue building line as evidence for the Group III packet. That particular project-specific change should not automatically transfer. Group III itself contains explicit blockfront concerns on page 4 and incorporated apartment-layout improvements on page 5, but those are different evidence. The broad sample should remain intact; claims need their own application scope. No source link, historical reference or score is changed in this trial.

Correct binary answers can still have weak explanations. For the ASPCA site, C 870156 ZSM, Luna returns a positive traffic obligation, agreeing with the earlier reference. Its label citation uses generic conformity to submitted plans, which the codebook explicitly excludes as a sufficient basis. The supplied declaration separately contains a specific 137-space cap, so stronger support is available in the same packet. In another evidence record, Luna labels the applicant's statement that it will try to locate affordable units in the requested area, if feasible, as an applicant promise, while its own explanation calls this qualified willingness and its binary affordability-obligation field is negative. Supplementary event statuses cannot yet be treated as validated data. Some declaration prose also says the agreement is recorded when the supplied text establishes approval and a requirement to record it, which are distinct stages.

Luna usefully leaves Whitestone's design obligation unresolved: the board attachment labels a 350-unit cap agreed by the developer, while the CPC discussion describes 415 units. That ambiguity deserves preservation. Its supplementary record, however, assigns an applicant-promise status to a community-board actor; a future schema should distinguish who reports a statement, who would undertake it, and who adopts it. The Sunset Park subsidy and lead-removal statements are additional boundary cases between proposed features and explicit undertakings. No favorable interpretation of those cases is added to the score.

Jacob authorized stopping Jev and trying Luna, not bulk relabeling. The Jev pilot runner now exits before credential handling or requests, and the already-paused corpus runner remains paused. The interrupted 12-statement Jev experiment has prepared inputs but no submitted calls. This Luna trial uses Codex account usage, not Vercel credits; exact per-run token or dollar charges are unavailable. Original human labels, previous model readings and production outputs remain unchanged.

The next recommendation is a fresh, bounded comparison on already human-coded reports, deliberately including more positive Council and civic examples and long reports. Preserve original human disagreements, check positive and negative answers, and inspect the action, actor, status and application behind each quoted claim. A short targeted check of the current misses can refine those rules, but should remain separate from fresh validation. This trial is sufficient to justify that next test, not to restart the full corpus.

Reproduction: source packets, codebook, prompt, hashes, dispatch metadata and raw readings are frozen under `data_raw/cpc_subagent_pilot/20260922_luna/`. Readers began without inherited conversation history and were instructed to read only their assigned packets and shared rules. Blinding is by instruction in a shared workspace. The requested model is `gpt-6-luna` with medium reasoning, not a pinned provider build. Saved readings reproduce downstream tables; future model execution is not deterministic. Run `make` from `tasks/audits/pilot_ulurp_cpc_llm_labels/code/`, then `make output/2026-09-22-luna-pilot.pdf` from `logbook/`. Ordinary Make launches no model calls.

Later on September 22, Jacob requested Sol subagents at medium and another test. Three GPT-6 Sol readers received the same four complete source packets, assignments, and 36-field codebook. The frozen reader instructions differ only in model and reader identifiers, and new readers received no earlier answers or targeted hints about mistakes. This is a closer comparison of models than the Jev comparison, although it remains one reading per model on four known development reports. No fresh validation is claimed.

Sol returns all four reports, 144 field judgments and 36 evidence records. Every positive label (47/47) and evidence record (36/36) has an exact source quotation. Sol agrees with 76 of the same 79 references, compared with Luna's 75. It recovers 30 of 33 reference positives and matches all 46 reference negatives. One reference positive is coded negative and two are unresolved. Across all 144 judgments, Sol leaves four unresolved versus Luna's one. The models agree on 135/144 fields. Sol matches three references Luna missed; Luna matches two that Sol leaves unresolved. These are not independent samples, and a one-decision net gain is not persuasive evidence of general superiority.

The useful improvements are concrete. Sol finds the explicit sanitation-capacity question in Commerce Avenue and the independent homeowners association's support in Whitestone Lanes. It also treats the Commerce Avenue street-tree undertaking as a character obligation, although both readers still reject the design-obligation interpretation in the reference. For the ASPCA site, the binary labels agree, but Sol cites the specific 137-space restriction in the incorporated declaration rather than generic conformity to submitted plans. Its supplementary affordable-housing record labels the applicant's feasibility-qualified willingness as unclear instead of calling it a promise. Those evidence improvements are not captured by the one-decision gain in the score.

Sol's additional uncertainty is concentrated in commitments. In Sunset Park it leaves the Section 8 subsidies and lead-paint removal unresolved, where Luna and the reference call them obligations. These deserve explicit coding rules: the report contains definite project language, but it appears within the project description. The codebook permits an undertaking integral to the original proposal and does not require a separately negotiated concession, so demanding an extra formal acceptance passage can also lose valid positives. In Whitestone, Sol also leaves the parking commitment unresolved because the board form asserts developer agreement while later CPC text describes different quantities and continuing discussions. Luna already flags the analogous unit-count conflict but gives the parking obligation a positive. Sol is more consistent about the conflict here, without establishing that every parking condition is unresolved.

Several differences have no frozen reference and therefore do not affect the reported score. Both readers recognize that Sunset Park Groups II and IV contain distinct project events, but Sol calls Group III's blockfront-preservation passage a proposal description, whereas Luna codes an explicit character/design concern. The wording calls preservation a paramount concern; the current codebook also excludes favorable preservation rationales without a concern or request. That overlap still needs a decision. Whitestone's income restriction pursuant to Mandatory Inclusionary Housing is another difference: Luna codes an obligation and Sol does not. The narrow definition excludes main-action approval and generic legal compliance alone, while the report describes a specific number of restricted units. Neither model's answer settles that boundary. Original model labels and reference readings remain unchanged after this review.

The next recommendation is to use Sol at medium for a small fresh trial, because it recovered two clear omissions and supplied better support for the long declaration. The observed advantage is modest, and Luna remains a plausible alternative; neither model's actual Codex cost was measured here. The reference set contains only one positive Council decision and two positive civic decisions, so it cannot establish reliability for those important fields. Include more already human-coded positive examples, check negatives as well as positives, and separate uncertainty about definitions from failures to read explicit evidence. No further reading or bulk relabeling is included in this comparison.
