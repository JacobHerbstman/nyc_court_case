Restoring the omitted answer rules substantially improves rejection of false claims in the fixed evidence audit, but it does not recover more usable positives. Across the same twenty passage-and-question pairs, the original checker yields nine correct usable judgments, five false positives, five false negatives and one unresolved answer. The corrected checker yields fourteen correct judgments, no false positives, three false negatives and three unresolved answers. Both versions confirm six of the twelve reference positives. The twenty reference readings were frozen before this repair; they are fallible Sol-medium source readings, not a human gold standard.

Looking only at the first predicate answer would give a more favorable result: agreement rises from 10/20 to 17/20. That is not the accuracy of the usable output. For commitments, the procedure also requires an applicant undertaking or an imposed condition. In three audited passages the corrected model says the substantive commitment is established but answers "neither" to its basis. Those contradictory answers remain unresolved. Two changed predicate answers therefore move previously missed positives to unresolved rather than recovered positives. The positive evidence should not be discarded, but this run does not establish that the automated checker can resolve it reliably.

The repair also does not remove every false positive outside those twenty audited pairs. In the completed Commerce Avenue report, C 190426 PCX, the model still treats the community board's requested parking conditions as an adopted obligation. The final CPC resolution does not adopt those conditions. This requested-versus-accepted distinction is a remaining substantive problem; it is separate from the confirmed transmission bug. Government and community-board testimony is no longer promoted to civic-group findings in the four reports with completed revised screening.

The isolated v14 test changes only what the verifier receives: each field's original answer criteria are appended verbatim to its instruction text, including in the basis question. The same nine source windows, question batches, answer options, model alias, screening answers, reference readings and aggregation rules are retained. All nine corrected requests return valid answers on their first attempt, with $0 observed account spending and $5 remaining. Saved original results reproduce byte for byte after the code change. This is a development repair on already inspected material, not fresh validation or proof of corpus-wide performance.

It keeps service missingness separate from semantic uncertainty and does not replace failed reports with a more convenient sample. The six remaining disagreements in the fixed passage audit come from three reports, not six independent reports. Five concern specific obligations and one concerns a design concern. Three are contradictory positive-predicate/negative-basis answers. They do not show that 70 percent of all CPC reports are correctly coded: this is a selected set of twenty routed passage-and-field checks against fallible model readings, with most revised reports still unavailable.

In Sunset Park, the companion report C 800194 HPK, page 5, says preserving the blockfront was a paramount concern and that the Commission ensured the new structure met the building line. Jev rejects the design concern and gives contradictory answers on character and design obligations. The concern wording is direct, but the reference classification of the building-line change as an imposed condition is debatable: the passage records an incorporated design improvement without presenting a separate formal condition. Our definitions put explicit concerns next to an exclusion for favorable preservation rationales, and put incorporated changes next to a stricter obligation construct. Those are coding distinctions we should preserve separately, rather than force into a single yes/no target. Original references remain unchanged; this interpretation does not increase the reported score.

In Commerce Avenue, C 190426 PCX, page 5, the applicant says trees will be planted as a buffer and provided along the street frontages. The same source produces a positive character obligation, a negative environmental obligation, and an unresolved design obligation because the second answer says neither an applicant undertaking nor an imposed condition exists. Different topics need not receive identical answers: whether landscaping alone meets the narrower environmental definition is itself a boundary to settle. But the positive design predicate followed by a negative basis is internally inconsistent. Even the positive character answer identifies both bases, although this passage explicitly supports the applicant undertaking. On page 4 the community board requests parking conditions; page 6 approves the acquisition without adopting those conditions. Jev still falsely treats the requested parking conditions as imposed. A single correct topic-level yes therefore does not establish correct actor or acceptance coding.

For the ASPCA site, C 870156 ZSM, the verifier receives pages 18--20 of companion C 861180 ZMM, containing the 137-space limit and related declaration provisions. The companion's page 11 expressly approves the signed declaration, and page 13 identifies its declarant; neither is in that verifier window. The focal report's page 8 also makes compliance with the declaration a condition of the special permit. The whole supplied report supports a conditional restriction, but the short excerpt does not include the full chain connecting the clause, the actor and acceptance. The frozen excerpt reference's definite applicant-undertaking label deserves reconsideration as well. This is a retrieval and reference-scope problem, not evidence that the whole-report answer should be no.

The next refinement should identify each concrete action once, distinguish what a speaker requested, what the applicant promised, what the deciding body required, and what the report says was incorporated, and only then attach topic labels. For example: "Does this statement say the applicant will plant these trees?" is more direct than asking for a qualifying accepted topic-specific obligation. For each selected statement, a single status choice can distinguish an advisory request, applicant promise, deciding-body condition, incorporated change, proposed feature, or unresolved status; mixed statements need separate records so requests are not overwritten by later acceptance. The final topic-stage labels can then be assembled in code. This is a proposed test, not a validated replacement or a change to production labels.

There are two additional limits to fix and test explicitly. First, a negative screening answer with no selected page receives no evidence check, so better rejection of false positives alone cannot recover everything missed at screening. A broad passage search must retain coverage of every report section, and known missed positives and a sample of negative answers must be checked. Second, declaration clauses need their identified approval and actor passages, not just adjacent pages. Retain explicit unresolved results when that link is absent. [TypeSafe's published limitations](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13), reviewed September 22, describe difficulty with indirection, distracting context and logical consistency across questions; these support testing simpler decisions, without proving the cause of any particular mistake or identifying the gateway's exact model version.

The earlier recovery sent 50--65 questions per request; its focused checks sent 1--10. All nine focused checks succeeded, whereas 59 of 62 larger requests failed. This suggests a separate test of smaller question batches with identical source text and definitions. Request size is not established as the cause: the saved provider response says only that the service is temporarily unavailable. Splitting questions, changing definitions and changing retrieved context should be evaluated separately, and no such experiment is silently mixed into the frozen retry.

Reproduction: run `make` from the existing pilot audit's code directory, then run the following from `logbook/`:

```sh
make output/2026-09-22-jev-rules-retry.pdf
```

The existing preparation, acquisition and comparison scripts handle the recorded variants; no new analysis task or separate pipeline is added. Ordinary Make performs no inference. The appendix contains the fixed-pair correction comparison as well as the twenty-report coverage accounting.
