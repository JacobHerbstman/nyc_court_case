Character, scale, and preservation

The broad scale_character_preservation field is not synonymous with opposition
to neighborhood change. It also covers substantive physical design, height,
bulk, density, and preservation issues. Continue to separate
neighborhood_character, scale_density_design, and historic_preservation.

Require a project-specific review issue: an actor's concern, question, request,
or substantive assessment about one of these topics. A supportive actor or CPC
can raise an issue. The explanation must identify the actor, the physical or
preservation question being assessed, and the relevant action. Do not infer
local opposition from CPC discussion or from the issue flag alone.

Neutral descriptions of dimensions, facade materials, the proposed design, or
surrounding buildings do not qualify simply because they were spoken at a
hearing. Nor do generic statements that development is appropriate, that land
can be developed, or that sale will maximize development potential. A concrete
question about a building's height or its buffer from neighboring homes does
qualify as scale/design; neighborhood_character additionally requires explicit
evidence about neighborhood fit or character, not just a dimensional answer.

Do not require the word "character" for the broad field. Actual requests to
change a cafe layout, building facade, streetwall, or height can qualify even
for a small project. Interior design or layout requires an actual review issue,
not a description of a rehabilitation program. Jobs, tax revenues, and industrial
employment alone do not establish physical character or historic preservation;
retain their documented opposition in the relevant actor/process fields.

A companion's issue counts only when it concerns the focal site's proposal or
shared review. Quote the connection. A common project title, vote date, or
supplied-source membership alone does not prove that it applies.

Revisions, commitments, and mitigation

Preserve the broad legacy meaning: a substantive application/project change
OR an explicitly undertaken or imposed project-specific commitment/mitigation.
This field alone does not establish a change made during ULURP or a concession
caused by local opposition. Do not optimize it to reproduce a human zero.

Before coding 1, identify what changed or was committed, who undertook or
imposed it, and the statement establishing that status. A request, suggestion,
or recommendation alone does not establish adoption. Generic promises to obey
the law and generic resolution conditions do not qualify. Neither does an
ordinary proposed feature without a documented mitigation/commitment purpose.

In the evidence explanation distinguish: adopted project change; undertaken
or imposed commitment/mitigation; request only; original project feature; or
procedural/administrative action. State whether the report establishes a new
change during review, a pre-existing commitment, or unknown timing. Do not infer
timing from the section in which a commitment is described. An actual binding
mitigation can meet the broad legacy field even when its timing is unknown;
describe that limit instead of calling it a newly negotiated concession.

Studies, outreach, monitoring, auction scheduling, and document processing alone
are not substantive revisions. A mandatory restriction on how parcels may be
developed or disposed of can qualify when the report explains its substantive
effect; do not exclude every disposition condition as administrative. A
modification to a separate area-wide proposal is not a revision to the focal
application. Preserve a documented related action in the explanation.

Use explicit_local_response only for an express link to a local request or
opposition, identifying the same event. A mitigation's existence, CPC's question,
or a change after a hearing does not by itself establish that link. Explain
conflicting or insufficient evidence rather than silently guessing its timing,
actor, application, or adoption status.
